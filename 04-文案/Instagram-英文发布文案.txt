Before you buy something to “finish” an outfit, try taking one competing detail away.

These six looks are useful for practising where the eye goes. Each full-length image is followed by a close-up, so you can study both the silhouette and the smaller decisions.

If two colours compete, change their proportions. Look 5 gives cobalt the coat and red only the small heart bag, with black underneath. Try that relationship using colours you already own.

If a statement skirt feels difficult to style, simplify above it. The pink skirt in look 1 gets a white shirt; the patterned skirt in look 6 gets a black knit. Start with the simplest top in your wardrobe and compare.

If a suit feels too predictable, keep the jacket and change the shape below it. Look 4 pairs the grey blazer with a moving pleated skirt. Fit and skirt length still need adjusting to your own body.

For quieter clothes, look closer at the details: repeated gold in look 2, continuous pale layers inside the oversized coat in look 3.

Take a full-length photo, change one element and take another. You don’t need these exact pieces to see which version reads more clearly.

Which would help your wardrobe most: a colour edit, a different silhouette or fewer accessories?
